How this works

What this app is for

You order something. The confirmation mail arrives. You hand that mail to PackTrack, it pulls out what it can — shop, items, pictures, the promised delivery date, the delivery service, the tracking number — and puts the order on your home screen. Then you tick things off as they actually turn up.

Getting a mail in — the main way

Open the confirmation mail in Mail. Select All, then Copy. Come back here, tap the big clipboard button at the bottom, tap inside the dashed box and Paste. Then Read the mail.

Pasting this way matters: it carries the product pictures across as well, which a plain-text share never does.

Getting a mail in — the one-tap way

There's an Apple Shortcut recipe under The one-tap Shortcut below. Once it's set up, the mail's share sheet has a “Send to PackTrack” entry that opens the app with the mail already in it. Faster, but iOS only hands a Shortcut plain text — so no pictures, and a very long mail can get clipped. Use it for the quick ones and paste for the ones you want pictures on.

What it looks for

Shop, order number, order date, every item in the order with its own picture, the total, the promised delivery date — a single day or a range like “zwischen dem 3. und 5. September” — the delivery service (DHL, Deutsche Post, Austrian Post, DPD, GLS, Hermes, UPS, FedEx, PostNL, TNT, Amazon and others), the tracking number, the tracking link straight out of the mail so tracking is one tap, and the pickup place if it's not coming to your door. German and English mails both work.

It always shows you what it found before saving, and says plainly what it couldn't find. Every single field is editable, so a miss is never a dead end.

The dispatch mail too

The same is true for re-reading a mail you've already sent in — to pick up pictures a older version missed, for instance. Choose to update the order you already have and the app fills in what's missing without duplicating anything, even if the item names have since been tidied up.

Send the shipping mail in the same way when it arrives. If the order number matches one you already have, PackTrack offers to update that order instead of making a second one — filling in the carrier, the tracking number and the sharpened delivery date.

Where it is — and the long press

A parcel goes through four states, and the app now keeps track of which one it is in: Ordered, On its way, Ready to collect, Arrived. This is a different question from when it is due, which is what the big number and the group headings answer.

Hold a card down on the home screen for half a second and it steps to the next state. While your finger is down the next state's colour sweeps across the card, so you can see what is about to happen — lift before it fills and nothing changes. When it does change, the whole card takes that state's colour: blue for on its way, violet for ready to collect, green for arrived, and the shop's own colour while it is only ordered. A small badge on the picture carries the same message as a symbol — a lorry, a map pin, a tick.

A card that steps all the way to Arrived ticks every item off for you, which is what arriving means; holding it once more takes it back round to Ordered and unticks them again. Every step raises an Undo button for a few seconds, and it puts the order back exactly as it was — including the dates on items you had already ticked by hand.

If holding is awkward, open the order: the Where it is strip near the top does the same job with a single tap, and it shows all four states at once so you can jump straight to the right one.

The timeline works the same way — hold a pill there and it steps along too.

One order, several parcels

Shops split deliveries all the time — Amazon most of all — so each item can carry its own state. On the order screen, tap an item to tick it off as before, and hold it to step that one item along: Ordered, On its way, Ready to collect, Arrived. The row takes that state's colour and gets its own badge, so a single order can plainly show one thing on its way, one waiting at the Paketshop and one already here.

Once any item carries a state of its own, the items drive the order — exactly as they already do for dates. The card on the home screen then shows the state of the parcel that is furthest along, because that is the one turning up next and the one there might be something to do about, and it writes the count beside it: “On its way · 1 of 3”.

Holding the order's own card still moves the whole order at once, and brings every outstanding item along with it. Undo puts every single item back exactly as it was.

The app reads the dispatch mail for it

When you paste in a mail that says the parcel has been sent — “Ihre Sendung ist unterwegs”, “versandt”, “has shipped”, “on its way” — the order is marked On its way by itself, and one that says it is waiting at a pickup point is marked Ready to collect. The review sheet tells you it did this before anything is saved.

A mail that only promises to tell you later — “wir informieren Sie, sobald Ihre Sendung unterwegs ist” — is not treated as a dispatch, which is why the order confirmation itself does not trip it. And a mail can only move an order forward: re-reading the old confirmation after the dispatch mail will not push it back to Ordered.

The home screen

One card per order, grouped into Late, Today, Coming and Arrived. The big number is days to go, and its colour is the message: red is overdue, amber is due today, green is all in. The card's own colour says where the parcel is (see above). The chips along the top filter the list.

The timeline

The button at the top right flips the home screen between the list and a timeline — a day-by-day strip of the days ahead. Each day shows which parcels are due to land on it, so you can see at a glance that Thursday is quiet and Friday has three things coming. Today is marked, weekends are dimmed, anything already overdue is banded at the top, and anything with no date is collected at the bottom.

When an order's items each carry their own date, each one appears on its own day with its name, so you can see exactly what lands when.

When a shop promises a window rather than a day — “between the 5th and the 7th” — the order is drawn on every day it might turn up on, outlined rather than filled, and labelled with the window so you know why it repeats.

The pickup place

If the mail says the parcel is going somewhere other than your door — a post office, a Paketshop, a Packstation, a collection point — PackTrack picks that line out and puts it on the order. You can also type it in yourself, and the app remembers the places you've used so the next one is a tap away.

The pickup place is a link: tap it on the order screen and it opens in Maps, ready to navigate to.

Each item its own date

Shops often promise different days for different things in one order — Amazon splits deliveries, and a shop may say one line ships now and another next week. Tick “Each item has its own delivery date” in the editor and every item gets its own date box.

Once items carry dates, they drive the order: the big number counts down to the next item still outstanding, the order only counts as late once the last one is overdue, and each item shows its own date on the order screen. Best of all, the timeline stops showing the order as one blob and puts each item on the day it actually lands, named. Untick the box and the order goes back to a single date for everything.

When the mail gives a delivery time, not a date

Plenty of shops promise “Lieferzeit 1–3 Werktage” instead of naming a day. When PackTrack spots that and no real date was given, the review sheet offers the arithmetic: it counts forward from the order date in working days only — skipping weekends and Swedish röda dagar, including midsommarafton, julafton and nyårsafton — and shows you the result. It is only used if you tap Use it; the app never quietly invents a delivery date. If a mail names several times (“…, mit Gravur 2–4 Werktage”) the widest span is taken, which is the honest one.

A date set this way is marked (estimated) on the order screen and with a ≈ on the card, so it never looks like a promise. Type a date yourself and the marking goes away.

Suggested follow-ups

The app knows the dates, so under Follow-ups it offers the two or three to-dos that actually follow from them — each with a date already worked out. Tap one to add it:

  • Chase the shop — three days after the promised date, or today if it is already overdue.
  • Return window closes — 14 days after the last item arrived, while you can still change your mind.
  • Register the warranty — a few working days after everything arrived, while the receipt is still to hand.

Follow-ups can carry a date of their own whether they were suggested or you typed them, and a dated one appears on the timeline on the day it falls due. One that slips past its date turns red on the order screen.

A link back to the mail

An order can hold a link straight back to the mail it came from. When one is present, the order screen shows Open the original mail under the tracking button, and tapping it jumps to that message.

If the mail you paste happens to contain a Spark link, PackTrack grabs it by itself. Otherwise you need to get one from Spark and paste it into Link to the mail in the editor — a Spark link looks like https://sparkmailapp.com/dpl/bl?token=…. Apple Mail's own message:// links work just as well.

Colours

Every shop gets its own clear, flat colour and keeps it — Amazon is always the same colour, Thomann is always another — so you recognise an order before you've read a word of it. On top of that the status colours the number: red when it's overdue, amber when it's due today, green when everything's in. Opening an order paints the whole screen in that shop's colour.

Ticking things off

Tap an order to open it. Each item has its own tick — Amazon splits deliveries, so this is how it should work. Tick items as they land; when the last one is ticked the whole order goes green and drops into Arrived. Nothing gets deleted, so the order stays as your record.

Follow-ups

Each order can carry your own to-dos — “return the small one”, “register the warranty”, “check the invoice”. They get their own ticks, and the Follow-ups chip on the home screen shows every open one across all your orders.

Tracking

If the mail carried a tracking link, the order screen shows a big Track this parcel button. If it only carried a number, PackTrack builds the right link itself for DHL, Austrian Post, DPD, GLS, Hermes, UPS, FedEx and PostNL.

Pictures

Product pictures come out of a pasted mail automatically — both from shops that link every product (Amazon) and from shops that just show a picture and a name in a row (most others). The app tries to store a copy on the device so it still shows offline; when the shop won't allow that, it keeps the link instead and the picture appears when you're online. You can also tap the little square next to any item in the editor and take or choose a photo yourself — those are always stored on the device.

Updates

The app checks for a new version when you open it (and when you come back to it, at most every 5 minutes). When one is live a bar appears at the bottom with an Update button — one tap loads it. Check for app update below asks immediately.

Backups

Under Your data you can save all your orders as a backup file, copy them to the clipboard, or restore. A restore shows its work: it tells you exactly how many orders came back, and a bad file leaves your live data untouched. The app also keeps its own daily snapshots on the device, and the Check the safety net works button proves that in front of you with real numbers.

Where your data lives

Everything is stored only on this device, inside this app. Nothing is synced or sent anywhere — which is exactly why the backup buttons exist.

The one-tap Shortcut

Build this once in the Shortcuts app and every confirmation mail is two taps away from being an order. It takes about three minutes.

1. Shortcuts → + → tap the shortcut's name → Details → switch on Show in Share Sheet. Under “Receive”, leave Text on (turn the rest off).

2. Add the action Text, and put Shortcut Input inside it (tap the field, then pick Shortcut Input from the variable bar).

3. Add the action URL Encode and point it at that Text.

5. Name it Send to PackTrack and save.

Now: open a confirmation mail → share → Send to PackTrack. The app opens with the mail already read, showing you what it found.

Remember: this route is plain text only, so no product pictures. Copy-and-paste is the one that brings pictures.

Your data

Your orders live only on this device. Every now and then, save a backup file you can keep safe or move to another phone.

The app's own safety net

Each day you open the app it quietly keeps a snapshot of your orders on this device — the last 10 are kept. Handy if something gets deleted by mistake. It is not a substitute for the export file above: snapshots live inside the app, so they go with it.
